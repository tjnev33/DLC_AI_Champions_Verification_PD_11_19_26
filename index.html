“I am an early childhood educator, and I need your help analyzing a kindergarten student's handwriting and fine motor progression. I am going to upload a series of writing samples.

Here is the context for this student:

  • Student Profile: [Insert brief description, e.g., Kindergarten student, struggles with fine motor strength].
  • Intervention/Scaffolding Used: [Insert any interventions, e.g., introduced sentence strips, highlighted baselines, 1:1 conversation about neatness in March].
  • Current Focus: [Insert what you are currently working on, e.g., phonics, finger spaces, lowercase letters].

Please do the following:

  1. Analyze the images: Review the uploaded writing samples and identify the trends in their fine motor control, spatial awareness, letter formation, word spacing, and executive functioning (self-correction).
  2. Compare 'Before' and 'After': Highlight the differences between their early baseline work and their recent work following my interventions.
  3. Identify Cognitive Shifts: Tell me what this physical progression means for their cognitive development (e.g., freed up working memory for phonics/spelling).
  4. Create a Google Doc: Once you have analyzed the data, please generate a structured Google Doc report summarizing this progress.

Please format the Google Doc with the following sections:

  • Overview of Progress
  • Baseline Profile (Include an HTML table with a dashed border as a placeholder for me to insert a 'Before' image).
  • Post-Intervention Profile (Include an HTML table with a dashed border as a placeholder for me to insert an 'After' image).
  • Comparative Data Analysis (Create a 3-column table comparing Skill Category, Baseline Observation, and Current Assessment).
  • Current Cognitive Shift / Next Steps”

How to use this: Start a new chat. Paste the prompt into the chat and fill in the bracketed info with the new student's details. Upload the new batch of pictures. Hit send.

Part 2 · Beyond Prompting
Why verification matters
Checking AI output has always mattered. It matters more now, for two reasons.

 

  
Why it matters more now

 

  
It looks finished
AI output arrives polished and confident. A weak draft and a strong one can look the same at a glance.

  
There's more of it
AI produces far more than anyone can check line by line, and more of it is reaching your desk already written.

 

 


 

  
The takeaway

  
The skill isn't checking everything. It's knowing how hard to test, based on the purpose, the audience and the stakes.

 


 

  
Every AI output is a draft

  

   
AI can helpBuild
Almost anything you make at work:
An emailA family newsletterA reportA data analysisA dashboardA presentationA surveyA websiteA video
→
   
AI can helpTest
  • Is it accurate?
  • Does it meet the goal or the success criteria?
  • If it makes claims, do they hold up?
→
   
Only youSign off
Use it, modify it, retest it or don't use it.

  

 


 

  
Four ways AI output fails

  

   
Volume
More output than anyone can realistically check. Length and polish hide gaps.
What actually matters for how I'll use it?

   
Hallucinations and mistakes
Confident claims, numbers or sources that are wrong or invented.
Could I confirm this somewhere independent?

   
Bias
One-sided framing, missing perspectives, assumptions stated as fact.
Whose view is centered, and whose is missing?

   
Sycophancy
The AI agrees with you or tells you what you hoped to hear.
Would it say the same if I'd asked the opposite?

Handing the test to students

  
Champions run a test at the end of every mission. Students can learn the same habit. The first time, AI drafts most of the verification plan and the student reviews it. Over time, students take over each decision.
